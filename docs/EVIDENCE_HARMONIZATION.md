# Global Evidence Network and Evidence Harmonization

Fynura maintains an allowlisted registry of global, regional and national public-health authorities. Registry membership describes the intended evidence ecosystem; it does not imply that an adapter is operational. Operational status is reported only after a production retrieval and extraction pathway has been verified.

## Multi-source does not mean sum every source

Reports describing the same event frequently overlap. Fynura never adds values merely because different organizations published them. Observations are matched using threat, indicator, geography and geographic level, reporting period/cutoff, case definition, classification, population and unit.

Each matched family is classified as the same observation family, compatible, complementary, conflicting or non-comparable. Source-reported values are preserved. Normalization standardizes identifiers, dates, units and terminology without rewriting values to force agreement.

## Canonical selection

Canonical selection is contextual. WHO is often appropriate for global comparability; a regional authority may be more appropriate for a regional question; and a national ministry may be more current and specific for a national outbreak. Selection considers compatible definitions, geographic specificity, authority for the requested scope, reporting cutoff, publication date and extraction confidence.

The canonical observation retains supporting, complementary and conflicting observations. A disagreement is not averaged away. Duplicate republications are not counted as independent corroboration.

## What should I cite?

Cite the original authority supporting the selected claim. Fynura exposes organization, source URL, publication date when available, reporting cutoff and retrieval timestamp. When metadata are absent, Fynura omits them rather than inventing them.
